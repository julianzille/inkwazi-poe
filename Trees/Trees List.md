# Trees List

Lebombo wattle (*Newtonia hildebrandtii*)
- canopy tree -- abundant in Sand Forest
- wine-red pods, flattened, elongated

Buffalo thorn (*Ziziphus mucronata*)
- spines paired -- one straight, one hooked
- 'buffalo' -- defensive behaviour of buffalos backing into tree when cornered by predators.

Lavender feverberry (*Croton gratissimus*)

Balloon thorn (*Vachellia luederitzii*)
- slender ant lays eggs in thorns
	- injects the thorn with growth hormone
	- symbiosis: reproductive housing for ants, trees protected from browsers (ants bite mammals)

Scented-pod thorn (*Acacia nilotica*)
- straight, white spines raked backward (outside tent)
- rough, fissured, grey-black bark
- pods beaded, red-brown

Num-num (*Carissa bispinosa*)
- forked spines (*bispinosa* -- "two spines")
- fruit eaten by people, monkeys, birds

Magic guarri (*Euclea divinorum*)
- *divinorum* -- "magic"
- wavy leaves

Ribbed currant (*Searsia pallens*)
- trifoliolate leaves
- orange lenticels

Thorny karee (*Searsia guenzii*)
- trifoliolate leaves (more shiny than ribbed currant)
- spiny stem

Tamboti (*Spirostachys africana*)
- Spirostachys: spiral + stachys (spike)
	- flowers arranged on tight spiral on a spike
	- 3-lobed fruit
- poisonous bark (+ smoke)
- nibbled on at base by porcupine
- dark brown, cracked bark
- seeds usually parasitised by Jumping Bean Moth larva

White stemmed guarri (*Euclea crispa*)
- whiteish silver stem

Sneezewood (*Ptaeroxylon obliquum*)
- leaves compound, opposite, 'lopsided' (*obliquum* = lopsided)
- sawdust causes severe sneezing
- incredibly smooth
- branches grow vertically 

Pepper bark (*Warburgia salutaris*)
- edible leaves -- harvested for food / tea
- medicinal properties
- highly endangered (overharvested)

Elephant's Pudding (*Cissus rotundifolia*)
- evergreen succulent vine

Torchwood / Green-thorn (*Balanites maughamii*)
- trunk looks like Olympic flame
- dry kernels burnt as torches (hence name)

Swazi ordeal (*Erythrophleum lasianthum*)
- large, round-crowned, drooping trees
- dark green, glossy leaves
- Sand Forest
- Used for trials by ordeal -- accused were forced to drink concoction of roots. Life or death verdict. 

Black Monkey Orange (*Strychnos madagascariensis*)
- unarmed 
- fruit pulp is bright yellow

Green Monkey Orange (*Strychnos spinosa*)
- armed with paired spines
- fruit pulp is brown

Spider Flower Poison Rope (*Strophanthus gerrardii*)
- alien, starfish like flower; demogorgon

Sausage tree (*Kigelia africana*)
- sausage-like fruit (~5 kg, ~60cm)
- blood-red flowers bloom in Spring

Sicamore Fig (*Ficus sycomorus*)
- pale brownish bark
- common along drainage lines

False Tamboti (*Cleistanthus schlechteri*)
- no milky latex that Tamboti has
- denser, paler bark
- not toxic

Umbrella thorn (*Vachellia tortilis*)
- paired thorns, with one longer than the other

Sticky thorn (*Vachellia borleae*)
- up to 5m tall
- new leaves sticky
- dark reddish brown bark; whitish, paired spines

Sand num-num (*Carissa tetramera*)
- smaller, narrower, less glossy leaves than num-num
- sandy, inland areas

Bushveld Saffron (*Elaeodendron transvaalense*)
- pale gray, smooth bark

Weeping Boer-Bean (*Schotia brachypetala*)
- up to 16m tall
- single thick trunk that branches low down
- flowers yield lots of nectar that 'weep' / drip from tree

Natal mahogany (*Trichilia emetica*)
- evergreen
- dark green glossy leaves

Candy striped Crinum (*Crinum stuhlmannii*)
- Aka candy striped vlei lily.
- Flourishes after rain.
