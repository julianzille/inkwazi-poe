2. GISS: general impression, size and shape of bird

3. Passerine: 
	- perching birds
	- anisodactyl feet (3 forward, 1 back)
	- gorgeous bush shrike, pink throated twinspot
	
	Near-passerine:
	- informal grouping of land-dwelling birds that share behavioral traits with passerines (eg altricial chicks), but lack anisodactyl foot structure. 
	- usually zygodactyl (2 forward, 2 back)
	- little bee-eater, trumpeter hornbill, kingfishers
	
	Non-passerine:
	- non-perching birds. Unique foot-structures specialised for swimming, walking, tearing meat.
	- eg crested guineafowl, batelier eagle

3. Altricial: chicks born blind and helpless Eg birds of prey
   Precocial: chicks born with feathers, can see and can walk. Eg. 3 banded plover, ostrich, hamerkop.

4. Ostrich:
	- didactyl foot (only two) -- minimize ground friction for high-speed running
	- broad bill with wide gape -- allows rapid, non-selective plucking of seeds and foliage, small invertebrates swallowed whole
	
	White-faced whistling duck:
	- webbed footing -- efficient for swimming
	- soft-edged, broad bill with comb like margins -- filter aquatic seeds
	
	Natal spurfowl:
	- heavy, blunt claws -- scratch open earth for seeds and soil invertebrates
	- short, stout beak -- curshing force to break tough seeds and plant matter 
	
	Black Sparrowhark:
	- long, muscular toes, sharp curved talons 
	- sharp, hooked upper mandible with acute cutting edge
	- both enable efficient hunting and eating 
	
	Purple crested turaco
	- flexible zygodactyl (2 front 2 back) toes -- exceptional grip and dexterity in foliage
	- short, stout bill

5. Trumpeter hornbills nesting:
	- nest in natural tree cavities high above ground.
	- female self-imprisonment -- seals herself inside using mud, faeces and fruit pulp
	- narrow feeding slit for male to pass food through -- male is sole provider
	- while sealed inside, female drops all her flight feathers, which grow back before release.
	- when chicks are partly grown, female breaks wall open. Both parents rebuild it to protect chicks until they fledge.

6. Brood parasites lay eggs in nests of other species, chicks hatch and are either reared by host bird, or evict host chicks from nest. Eg Cuckoos (red-chested cuckoo parasitises robin-chats, klaas's cuckoo, greater honeyguide parasitises bee-eaters, kingfishers)

7. Owls and nightjars sight at night:
	- have oversized eyes relative to skull, collecting maximum ambient light. Pupils dilate extremely wide
	- their retinas contain a vast majority of rod cells (which detect low light/monochrome but no colour) than cone cells (for colour)
	- reflective layer behind retina that bounces light back through photoreceptors, doubling light absorption

8. Vultures detecting small carcasses:
	- vultures have much better eyesight (deep fovea with high densities of visual receptors)
	- vultures watch each other 
	- have a wide field of view
	
	Owls hearing:
	- have asymmetrical ear openings -- positioned higher on the skull than the other, allowing them to measure time and volume differences vertically as well as horizontally
	- facial feathers act as a sound dish, directing acoustics to ear canals
	- heavily expanded brain region dedicated to processing sound
	
9. How birds navigate migrations:
	- celestial navigation: use the position of the sun during the day and star constellations at night
	- geomagnetic sensing: able to detect Earth's magnetic field lines (which run North-South)
	- use coastline boundaries, major river systems, topography
	
	**Palaearctic Migration:** Long-distance migration between Europe/Asia (Palaearctic region) and Southern Africa. Eg Barn Swallow 
	**Intra-African Migration:** Seasonal movements strictly within the African continent, often following rainfall patterns. Eg southern carmine bee-eater
	**Altitudinal Migration:** Short-distance movements up and down mountain elevations driven by local weather or food shifts. Eg. Drakensberg Rockjumper
	**Seasonal Migration:** Movements corresponding directly to wet/dry seasons, usually for breeding or food availability within a region. Eg yellow-billed kite
	  
	 Migration vs nomadism: 
	- migration is predictable mass movement between fixed breeding and non-breeding grounds. 
	- nomadism is irregular, unpredictable movements in response to localised resources without set routes

10. 5 methods of personal care for birds:
	- preening: distribute preen wax from specialised glans across feathers with beak to clean and waterproof plumage
	- bathing: water/dust splashing removes excess oil, parasites etc
	- anointing: rubbing live ants onto feathers -- the formic acid secreted acts as an insecticide/fungicide
	- sunbathing: spreads wings in warm sunlight encourages preen oil absorption and forces parasites to move
	- allopreening: mutual grooming between pair members

11. &Beyond Star Bird programme:
	- each game reserve identifies a curated checklist of iconic, rare, or endemic bird species specific to that region (the "Star Birds").
	- gamifies birdwatching for guests of all experience levels and provides trained safari guides with a structured tool to highlight region-specific biodiversity beyond just the "Big Five"
