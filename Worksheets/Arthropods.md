4. *Why don't spiders get stuck in their own webs?* Not all silk is sticky -- spiders avoid walking on sticky silk (for catching prey). In addition, spiders have moveable claws on feet that grip and release the web's thread as they walk.

9. Ametabolic lifecycle is where the insect takes up one distinct body form. The young that hatch from eggs look like miniature versions of the adults (no nymphs or pupae). They moult several times until adult size is attained. Eg springtails, bristletails, fishmoths.
   Hemimetabolic (incomplete) metamorphosis is the lifecycle consisting of egg, nymph and aduls, but no pupae. The nymph may (type A) or may not (type B) resemble the adults in appearance and lifestyle. Type A examples: cockroaches, bugs, locusts, grasshoppers, web-spinners, stick insects. Type B (generally live near water): dragonflies, damselflies. 
   Holometabolic (compete) metamorphosis consists of egg, larva, pupa and adult. At each stage, the insect takes up a distinct life form (unique appearance and diet / behaviour). Eg bees, ants, wasps, beetles, moths, butterflies.
