# Features
- Square, wide mouth adapted for short grass grazing; high neck hump containing nuchal ligament to support heavy head
- Keratinous front and rear horns (no bony core)

# Behaviour
- Wallowing in mud to regulate body temperature and coat skin against biting flies and ticks
- Communal dung middens (middens scraped and scattered by territorial bulls)
- Calf walks in front of the mother (unlike black rhino where calf follows behind)

# Social Structure
- More social than black rhino: females and subadults form loose "crashes" of up to 6-10
- Adult bulls are strictly territorial (occupying small, well-defined territories); tolerate non-territorial subordinate bulls as long as submissive posture is shown
- Subadult calves ejected at 2-3 years prior to next birth; ejected calves pair up with peers or attach to another cow-calf group

# Food & Diet
- Pure selective bulk grazers, mowing short perennial grass carpets (*Themeda*, *Cenchrus*)

# Reproduction & Offspring
- 16 month gestation; females calve at ~6 years; bulls reach social/breeding maturity at ~10-12 years
- Cow seeks dense cover to give birth; calves stand within an hour and remain in front of the mother

# Ecology
- Megaherbivore lawn mower: creates and maintains short grazing lawns that act as natural firebreaks and provide forage for smaller antelopes
