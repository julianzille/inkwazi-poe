# Features
- Age largely determined by teeth -- will go through 6 sets (last erupts at 30 years). Once warn out, will spend rest of life in areas where vegetation is soft
- age inferred by combination of
	- size + size of tusks (although largely genetic)
	- indentation above + behind eyes
- gender:
	- male have more rounded facial profile; female more square![[Pasted image 20260830112636.png|361]]
# Food
- outcompete most herbivores -- bark, fruit, roots, leaves, grasses
- molars grind forward and backward (not side to side)
- eat 4-6% of body weight daily (5000kg bull eats 300kg). Surprisingly little for such a large animal. 
- feed 16hrs / day; can go days without drinking
- drinks 200l per day (19l per trunk)
# Social structure
- matriarchal with herds of related female; males separate in herds or alone
- matriarch (largest cow) sets pace, activity and direction
- males leave herd at adolescence (12-13 years). Separation is gradual -- adolescents may follow herd at distance. Female intolerance of boisterousness of sexual maturity
- after independence, bulls alternate between bachelor herds and wandering alone
- bulls wander more than cows (cover more ground on day-to-day)
# Behaviour
- when disturbed, group clusters around matriarch
- altruistic: often help injured (although old/feeble females abandoned by the herd)
# Communication
- infrasound rumbling of stomach at low frequency, over several kms (up to 16km)
# Reproduction + offspring
- conception at ~10 years; birth interval 4-9 years; gestation 22 months
- males check females reproductive status by smell (trunk near genital)
- during estrus, female more wary of bulls. If persistently approached by bull, she will move away and circle back to herd later. If bull gets within touching distance, will mount.
- bulls reach *social* maturatity at ~38yrs
- cow gives birth close to family
- strong maternal bond
- weaned after 2-3 years; related calves suckle  
- unlike most antelope, calves do not bump the mothers mammaries when they are drinking. This is because mammary glands are located on chest between front legs
- at watering holes, female and calf in-and-out; males drink, wallow, swim

# Musth
- *"mischievous"* in Hindu
- occurs in younger bulls (~14-24 years) sporadically -- often suppressed by more mature bulls. Older bulls from 30 years old, yearly for 2-5 months at a time
- heightened testosterone -- looking for female mating partner
- dribble masks from urine
- sticky secretion from eyes stains cheeks
- when large bull in musth stays with a herd, usually means he is in contortship with estrous female
- female in oestrus knows how to call musth mature, powerful bulls individually
