# Features
- Males have larger, more prominent bosses
- Older buffalos' horns are larger and more curved

# Behaviour
- Graze roughly as much during day as during night, interspersed with resting and ruminating
- During day, graze in early morning and late evening; ruminate and rest in shady thicket during midday

# Social Structure
- Herds split up in Summer when resources are abundant; congregate in Winter when resources are more limited
	- In dry season (Winter), when no mating takes place, subadult prime bulls separate into bachelor herds
	- Herds tend to be largest during rains, especially near the end during peak mating season
- Stable groups of cows that are arranged in a dominance hierarchy, to which are attached adult and subadult bulls, ranked according to age and dominance
- Maternal bond with males ends after adolescence (3 years), after which they form bachelor groups. Cows maintain maternal bond until they become mothers themselves (3-4 years)
- Herd formation dynamics:
	- *P* (pathfinders) lead the way, not necessarily most dominant bulls, take turns
	- *I* (invalid) sick / old bulls
	- *BH* (basic herd)
	- *B* (bachelor male)

# Food & Diet
- Drink at least once every 24hrs
- Graze 5-80cm grass

# Reproduction & Offspring
- Females calve first at about 5 years
- 11.5 months gestation
- Bulls monitor female reproductive status by licking vulva, triggering urine tasting (flehmen response)
- Cow coming into heat is guarded by dominant bull
- Calves feeble for several hours, weak/slow for several weeks; protected by herd defensive circle

# Ecology
- At watering holes, large herds churn mud which promotes nutrient cycling and widening of waterholes
- Bulk grazers: reduce grassland to height preferred by more selective grazers
