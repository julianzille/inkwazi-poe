# Features
- male have larger, more prominent bosses
- older buffalos' horns are larger and more curved
# Social structure
- herds split up in Summer when resources are abundant; congregate in Winter when resources are more limited. 
	- In dry season (Winter), when no mating takes place, subadult prime bulls separate into bachelor herds
	- herds tend to be largest during rains, especially near the end during peak mating season
- stable groups of cows that are arranged in a dominance hierarchy, to which are attached adult and subadult bulls, ranked accordning to age and dominance
- maternal bond with males ends after adolescence (3 years), after which they form bachelor groups. Cows maintain maternal bond until they become mothers themselves (3-4 years)
- *P* (pathfinders) lead the way, not necesseralily most dominant bulls, take turns. *I* (invalid) sick / old bulls. *BH* (basic herd). *B* (bachelor male)

# Ecology
- at watering holes, large herds churn mud which promotes nutrient cycling and widening of hole
- reduce grassland to height preferred by more selective grazers
# Food
- drink at least once every 24hrs
- graze 5-80cm grass
- graze roughly as much during day as during night, interspersed with resting / ruminating
- during day, graze in early morning / late evening, ruminate / rest in shady thicket during midday
# Reproduction
- females calve first at about 5 years
- 11.5 months gestation
- bulls monitor female reproductive status by licking vulva, triggering urine tasting
- cow coming into heat is guarded by dominant bull
- calves feeble for several hours, weak/slow for several weeks. Herd defensive strategy.
