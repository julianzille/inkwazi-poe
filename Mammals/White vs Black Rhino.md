# Features & Morphology
- **Mouth structure**: White rhino has a wide, square flat lip (*Ceratotherium simum*, Dutch *wijd* = wide) for grazing; Black rhino has a hooked, prehensile pointed upper lip (*Diceros bicornis*) for browsing thorny twigs.
- **Head carriage**: White rhino holds head low near the ground; Black rhino holds head upright at shoulder level.
- **Hump & Ears**: White rhino has a pronounced nuchal hump and tubular, elongated ears; Black rhino lacks a prominent neck hump and has rounded trumpet-like ears.

# Tracks & Identification
- **Heel lobe**: White rhino track has a pronounced, deep indentation / back lobe; Black rhino track has a flat or gently indented rear pad.
- **Size**: White rhino tracks are significantly larger (length ~28-30cm vs ~20-22cm).
- **Negative space**: White rhino tracks show less "negative space" between the three distinct rounded toes; Black rhino shows more pronounced spacing.

# Behaviour & Ecology
- **Diet**: White rhino is an obligate short-grass grazer; Black rhino is a selective browser eating woody shrubs, succulents, and forbs.
- **Calf movement**: White rhino calves walk ahead of mother; Black rhino calves run behind the mother through dense bush.
- **Temperament**: Black rhinos are known to be far more inquisitive, reactive, and prone to mock or direct charges when surprised.
