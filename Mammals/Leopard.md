# Features
- Solitary, elusive felid with rosette-patterned coat adapted for camouflage

# Behaviour
- Nocturnal and crepuscular habits; highly arboreal cache behavior
- While moving with cubs, tail is curled upwards showing white underside as a follow-me signal
- Scent marking with urine and cheek-rubbing to delineate territory

# Social Structure
- Strictly solitary except for mating pairs and mothers with cubs

# Food & Diet
- Opportunistic carnivore (antelope, baboons, birds, small mammals)

# Reproduction & Offspring
- 3 month gestation; birth interval 1.5 years; sexually mature after ~2 years
- Cubs may be left hidden for several days while mother goes on foraging trips; mother leads cubs to kill after securing first feed
- Cubs moved every 2-3 days to new shelters to evade hyaenas and lions
- Suckling ceases after ~3 months
- At 9.5 months, join mother on hunts; at 11 months, make first kill; at 13 months gain independence (siblings stay together for first 2-3 months)

# Ecology
- Key apex predator controlling medium-sized ungulate and baboon populations

# Field Sightings
- **10 August** (~7:30am): Male leopard. Early morning scent marking near Sodwana Bay Road.
