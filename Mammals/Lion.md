# Features
- Largest African carnivore
- Male size and mane are inhibiting factors in hunting; pronounced sexual dimorphism driven by competition to monopolize female prides (*"the opportunity to monopolize reproduction of a whole group of females is behind the pronounced sexual dimorphism seen in lions, alone of all cats"* - Estes)

# Behaviour
- Rest for vast majority of day -- conserving energy and digesting
- Late afternoon and evening hunting activity
- Hunt at short-range -- stalking, laying low
- Co-operative hunting: some lie low while others encircle prey, driving it into ambush

# Social Structure
- Matriarchal prides:
	- No rigid rank hierarchy amongst females
	- Males evicted at 2.5 years, partly due to intolerance of older pride males
	- Each sex defends territory against intruders of same sex (males defend against males, females against females)
- Male coalitions:
	- Once evicted, young males enter transient nomad phase
	- Related males usually team up
	- Diminishing returns with large coalitions: difficult to co-ordinate group and defend multiple prides
- Takeovers and infanticide:
	- When new males take over a pride, they kill cubs <1 year old
	- Mothers sometimes defend or temporarily leave pride with cubs; if cubs are killed, lioness returns to estrus within a few weeks

# Food & Diet
- Carnivorous apex predator
- Food sharing dynamics:
	- Males feed first and aggressively, even when not participating in the hunt
	- Mothers eat before cubs

# Reproduction & Offspring
- Females breed at 4 years; males at 5 years
- 3.5 month gestation period; ~2 year birth interval
- Synchronized reproduction: lionesses synchronize cycles to form crèches where cubs are raised communally with cross-suckling
	- If pride already has cubs ~3 months old, mother delays reintroducing new cubs to minimize competition
- Mating priority:
	- First-come, first-served between coalition males -- pride males race to be first at estrous female's side, losers keep distance to prevent coalition-destroying in-fighting
	- Female in estrus for 2-4 days; mates hundreds of times (often with multiple coalition males to confuse paternity and maximize defense)
- Cubs:
	- Born in well-hidden lair; helpless for first 2 weeks
	- At 2 months, mother leads them to kills; weaned at 8 months
	- Mother does not slow pace for cubs >5 months old (abandoned if unable to keep up)

# Ecology
- Apex predator; regulates ungulate populations and structures herbivore distribution across savanna and woodland
