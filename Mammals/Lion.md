# Features
- largest African carnivore
- male size and mane are inhibiting factors in hunting

# Social Structure
- matriarchal prides
	- no rank hierarchy amongst females
	- males evicted at 2.5yrs, partly because of intolerance of other males
	- each sex defends part of the pride range against intruders of same sex (ie male members defend against male intruders)
- male coalitions
	- once evicted, young males are in unsettled transient phase
	- related males usually team-up
	- diminishing returns in size of coalition: difficult to co-ordinate group; difficult to defend multiple prides
- when males take over a pride, they will first kill any cubs <1 year old. Mothers will sometimes defend and/or leave pride with cubs. If her cubs are killed, she will come into heat within a few weeks.

# Behaviour
- rest for vast majority of day -- conserving energy, digesting
- late afternoon / evening hunting activity
- hunt at short-range -- stalking, laying low
- co-operative hunting: some will lie low while others encircle prey, hoping it flees in their direction
- food sharing:
	- males are most piggish, even though they don't usually participate in hunt
	- mothers will eat before cubs
# Reproduction
- females begin breeding at 4; males at 5
- 3.5 month gestation period; birth interval ~2 years
- females somewhat synchronise reproductive cycles -- creche, cubs raised collectively, communally. 
	- if pride already has cubs that are ~3 months, she will wait to reintroduce them to the pride (competition)
- core feature: first-come first-serve between dominant pride males. Instead of competing over a female in heat, pride males race to be first at her side -- losers keep their distance.
	- pride males share equal ownership over territory
	- female stays in estrus for 2-4 days; whoevers first stays with her for this duration (although females known to copulate with multiple males)
	- in-fighting would destroy the coalition
- hundreds of copulations:
	- females will mate with multiple pride males to "trick" all into thinking they're his cubs (more protection)
- cubs born in well-hidden lair, tiny. 
	- Helpless for 2 weeks. 
	- At 2 months, mother begin leading them to kills. 
	- Weaned at 8 months. 
	- mother will not slow her pace for cubs older than ~5 months old -- will often abandon if too weak to keep up
	- cubs have best chance of surviving when births of several females are synchronised. Access to milk from each others mothers

- *"the opportunity to monopolize reproduction of a whole group of females is behind the pronounced sexual dimorphism seen in lions, alone of all cats"* (Estes) -- because female lions live together in groups, a single male can control and mate with all of them if he dominates the pride. This huge reward drove male lions to evolve much larger bodies and thick manes so they can beat out rival males in fights.
